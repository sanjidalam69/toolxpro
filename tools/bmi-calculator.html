🩺 What is Body Mass Index (BMI)?

Body Mass Index (BMI) is a standardized anthropometric measurement developed by Belgian statistician Adolphe Quetelet. It provides a simple numeric screening indicator to categorize individuals based on their tissue mass (muscle, fat, and bone) relative to their stature. Medical practitioners, insurance providers, and public health organizations worldwide use BMI to assess population-level cardiometabolic risk.

📐 Mathematical Formulas for BMI Calculation

The calculation requires only two variables: weight and height. Depending on your preferred unit system, the formula is expressed as:

International Metric Formula:

BMI = Weight (kg) / [Height (m)]² Example: 70 kg ÷ (1.75 m × 1.75 m) = 22.86 (Normal)

Imperial Formula (US System):

BMI = [Weight (lbs) / (Height (in))²] × 703 Example: (154 lbs ÷ 69 in²) × 703 = 22.74 (Normal)

❓ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q: What are the main limitations of BMI?

BMI cannot differentiate between body fat and lean muscle tissue. For bodybuilders, strength athletes, and pregnant women, BMI may overestimate body fat. Conversely, in elderly individuals with muscle loss (sarcopenia), BMI may underestimate visceral fat.

Q: Does Asian population BMI criteria differ?

Yes. The WHO Asian cutoff points are lower due to higher risk of diabetes and cardiovascular disease at lower body weights: Normal is 18.5 – 22.9, Overweight is 23.0 – 27.4, and Obese is 27.5+.

Q: How can I reach a healthy BMI?

Focus on a balanced whole-food diet rich in dietary fiber and lean protein, engage in 150 minutes of aerobic exercise weekly, incorporate resistance training, stay hydrated, and ensure 7-8 hours of quality sleep.
