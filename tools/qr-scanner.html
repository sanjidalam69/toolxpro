🔍 How Browser-Based Optical QR Decoding Works

Traditional QR scanning required downloading native mobile applications from third-party app stores, which frequently requested unnecessary system permissions. With modern WebRTC (Web Real-Time Communication) and HTML5 Canvas APIs, optical barcode reading occurs securely inside your browser window without installing any executables.

🛡️ Safety Precautions Against QR Phishing ("Quishing")

Cybercriminals sometimes paste deceptive QR stickers over legitimate restaurant or parking meter codes. Our scanner prioritizes user security by displaying the complete decoded payload before opening:

  1. Inspect the Domain: Always verify that the top-level domain matches the legitimate company before clicking or entering credentials.
  2. Beware of Shortened URLs: Exercise caution if the decoded text points to unfamiliar link shorteners (such as bit.ly or tinyurl) in public physical locations.
  3. Check for SSL/TLS: Confirm that the destination URL begins with https:// rather than unencrypted http://.
  4. Review WiFi Credentials: When connecting to networks via QR codes, ensure the network name (SSID) matches your official venue.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q: Can I scan a damaged or poorly lit QR code?

Yes. QR codes feature built-in Reed-Solomon error correction algorithms, allowing the scanner to reconstruct lost pixels if between 7% and 30% of the pattern is obscured.

Q: Does this scanner support barcodes like UPC or EAN-13?

Our engine is primarily optimized for 2D QR matrix codes, Micro QR codes, and Data Matrix standards, which are standard for modern web and mobile communications.

Q: Are camera permissions stored permanently?

Camera permissions are managed exclusively by your browser's security sandbox. The video stream is immediately terminated when you click "Stop Camera" or close the browser tab.
