📱 What is a Quick Response (QR) Code?

Invented in 1994 by Masahiro Hara of Denso Wave, the Quick Response (QR) code is a two-dimensional matrix barcode capable of encoding up to several hundred times more alphanumeric data than conventional linear UPC barcodes. Modern smartphones feature native optical camera scanners that instantly parse and trigger web links, digital business cards, and payment requests.

⚙️ Key Anatomical Elements of a QR Code

  1. Finder Patterns (Positioning Eyes): The three distinctive square concentric boxes located at the top-left, top-right, and bottom-left corners help camera sensors detect orientation and angle of perspective.
  2. Timing Patterns: Alternating black and white module lines between finder patterns establish the coordinate grid spacing.
  3. Reed-Solomon Error Correction: Built-in mathematical redundancy allows the barcode to remain fully readable even if up to 30% of the surface area is scratched, obscured, or torn.
  4. Quiet Zone: The essential blank margin surrounding the outer perimeter that prevents background graphics from interfering with optical decoding.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q: What is the recommended contrast ratio for custom colored QR codes?

Always maintain high contrast between the dark foreground code and the light background (e.g., black or dark navy against crisp white). Avoid inverted light colors on dark backgrounds as some older scanner lenses struggle with negative polarity.

Q: What is the minimum recommended physical print size?

For standard short URLs (under 40 characters), a minimum print size of 2 cm × 2 cm (0.8 in × 0.8 in) is recommended for reliable smartphone scanning at standard reading distance.

Q: Is there any scan count limit?

No limit. These are static, non-expiring QR codes with unlimited lifetime scans.
