How to Calculate BMI & Understand Results

Published on July 21, 2026 • 5 min read

Body Mass Index, or BMI, is a quick and widely used medical screening tool to check if a person has a healthy ratio of weight to height. While it doesn't measure body fat percentage directly, it correlates strongly with metabolic health risks.

📐 The Mathematical Formula for BMI

Calculating your BMI is straightforward. The formula differs depending on whether you use the metric system or the imperial system:

Metric System (Standard):

BMI = Weight (kg) / [Height (m)]²

Imperial System (US Standard):

BMI = [Weight (lbs) / (Height (in))²] × 703

📊 Understanding BMI Classifications

According to the World Health Organization (WHO), adults over 20 years old are grouped into the following categories based on their BMI score:

BMI Range Classification Health Risk Level
Below 18.5 Underweight Moderate Risk
18.5 – 24.9 Normal Weight Minimal Risk (Healthy)
25.0 – 29.9 Overweight Increased Risk
30.0 and Above Obesity High Clinical Risk

🌏 South Asian BMI Criteria: Why Cutoffs are Lower

Clinical epidemiologists from the World Health Organization (WHO) and Harvard Medical School discovered that individuals of South Asian descent (Bangladeshis, Indians, Pakistanis) carry a higher percentage of internal abdominal visceral fat at lower body weights. Consequently, the Asian-Pacific classification defines lower danger cutoffs:

Classification Standard Western Cutoff South Asian / Asian Cutoff
Underweight < 18.5 < 18.5
Normal / Healthy Weight 18.5 – 24.9 18.5 – 22.9
Overweight 25.0 – 29.9 23.0 – 24.9
Obese ≥ 30.0 ≥ 25.0

📏 Beyond BMI: Waist-to-Height Ratio (WHtR)

Because BMI cannot distinguish between lean muscle and harmful visceral adipose tissue around vital organs, doctors recommend calculating your Waist-to-Height Ratio (WHtR) alongside BMI:

The Golden Cardiometabolic Rule:

"Keep your waist circumference to less than half your height" (WHtR < 0.50). If your waist is 34 inches, your height should be at least 68 inches (5 feet 8 inches) to minimize insulin resistance and hypertension risk.

🔥 Connecting BMI to Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) & Daily Calories (TDEE)

While BMI provides your physical classification, achieving your target weight requires understanding your energy expenditure. The Mifflin-St Jeor Equation calculates the baseline calories your body consumes simply remaining alive at rest:

Men: BMR = (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) - (5 × age in years) + 5

Women: BMR = (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) - (5 × age in years) - 161

Multiplying your BMR by your physical activity level (1.2 for sedentary desk work up to 1.725 for intense exercise) yields your Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE). To safely transition from an overweight BMI category to a normal category, maintain a mild caloric deficit of 300 to 500 kcal below your TDEE per day, targeting 0.5 kg of fat loss weekly without sacrificing muscle mass.

💡 Tips to Maintain a Healthy BMI

  1. Eat a Balanced Diet: Focus on whole foods, vegetables, fruits, lean proteins, and complex carbohydrates. Limit ultra-processed sugars and trans-fats.
  2. Stay Physically Active: Aim for at least 150 minutes of moderate aerobic exercises (like brisk walking or cycling) and 2 days of strength training weekly.
  3. Prioritize Sleep and Hydration: Chronic sleep deprivation increases cortisol and ghrelin levels, leading to increased visceral fat storage.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q: Why do bodybuilders have a high BMI despite low body fat?

Dense skeletal muscle weighs more per unit volume than adipose fat. BMI only measures raw mass divided by height squared, so highly muscular athletes will be categorized as "overweight" or "obese" despite having visible abdominal muscles.

Q: Can BMI be used for children and teenagers?

Children undergo rapid skeletal growth spurts. Pediatricians evaluate BMI percentiles (BMI-for-age growth charts from the CDC/WHO) rather than fixed adult numeric cutoff scores.

Q: How do I calculate my Ideal Body Weight (IBW)?

Using the Devine Formula: For adult men, 50 kg + 2.3 kg × (height in inches - 60). For adult women, 45.5 kg + 2.3 kg × (height in inches - 60).

Q: What is the difference between subcutaneous fat and visceral fat?

Subcutaneous fat is the pinchable layer beneath your skin. Visceral fat is deep internal fat surrounding the liver, intestines, and heart. High visceral fat secretes inflammatory cytokines that promote atherosclerosis and insulin resistance, which is why waist circumference measurements are critical.

Q: How often should I calculate and track my BMI?

Daily weigh-ins often fluctuate by 1-2 kg due to hydration, sodium intake, and digestive waste. For accurate health tracking, calculate your BMI once every two to four weeks under consistent morning conditions (fasting, after using the restroom).
